# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Resumake is for job seekers, students, researchers, and professionals who want a polished resume without writing LaTeX by hand. They are usually editing under practical constraints: quick iteration, reliable export, and enough control to tailor sections, order, and templates for a specific application.

## Product Purpose

The product helps users create elegant resumes from structured form data, choose a template, preview the result, and export the resume as PDF, JSON, or LaTeX source. Success means a user can start from the landing page, enter simple resume information, preview changes quickly, and leave with a usable document without needing to understand the rendering engine.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, capable, and precise. The interface should feel like a focused document tool: direct, dependable, and calm while still feeling modern enough for repeated use.

## Anti-references

Avoid marketing-heavy resume-builder patterns that hide the editor behind upsells, decorative dashboards, or oversized onboarding. Avoid interfaces that require server-side accounts for basic editing/export. Avoid making LaTeX compilation the only viable preview path, especially on lower-power devices.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the resume as the center of gravity.
2. Make the fast path fast: HTML preview should support immediate editing and lightweight PDF printing.
3. Keep LaTeX as a high-fidelity export path, not a blocker for ordinary previewing.
4. Preserve user control over sections, order, templates, and export formats.
5. Prefer transparent, local-first behavior where possible so users understand what is being generated and downloaded.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The editor should target accessible product UI basics: keyboard-usable controls, clear focus states, readable contrast, and responsive behavior that works on desktop and mobile browsers. Motion should be minimal and nonessential. Export workflows should not depend on drag-only interactions or high-performance devices.
